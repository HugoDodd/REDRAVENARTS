Adebisi Fabunmi

1945–2023

Artist Biography

Adebisi Fabunmi was born in Takoradi, Ghana, to Nigerian Igbo parents. He moved to Oshogbo, Nigeria, and in 1964 attended a historic art workshop led by Georgina Beier, launching his career as a core figure of the Oshogbo Art School. Primarily celebrated for his intricate linocut and woodcut prints, Fabunmi was also a curator at the Museum of Antiquities in Oshogbo and worked across diverse media, including oil paintings and yarn/wool tapestry painting.

Fabunmi’s iconography drew heavily from traditional Yoruba mythology, townscapes, and cultural motifs. He held numerous solo exhibitions in Nigeria, as well as shows in Kenya and a major retrospective in Bayreuth, Germany (1989). Internationally, his work was featured in seminal group exhibitions including Contemporary Art from Africa at the ICA in London (1967), alongside shows in New York, Vienna, and Prague.

His prints were exhibited at the Smithsonian’s National Museum of African Art in Washington, D.C. (2024–2025) in Bruce Onobrakpeya: The Mask and the Cross, where his work was juxtaposed with four other major Nigerian contemporaries. His art was also showcased in Nigerian Modernism at Tate Modern in London (2025–2026).
