Albert Mongita

1916–1985

Artist Biography

Albert Likeke Mongita, also known as Mongita Likeke, was a pioneering actor, dramatist, painter, theatre director and filmmaker in the Democratic Republic of Congo, both before and after its independence from Belgium. He was born in Irebu in Equatorial Province but his family relocated to Leopoldville (now Kinshasa) when he was an infant. He worked as a teacher and in the post office before in 1949 joining Radio Conge Belge as an editor and announcer. In the later 1940s and 1950s Mongita wrote a number of plays, directed various theatre productions and under the auspices of the Congo Film Club (set up in 1950) shot one of the first ever African films, The Film Lesson, in 1951. He became Director of the National Theatre and, on independence in 1960, Director of Cultural Affairs in Congo. In 1963 he directed a further film, a documentary.

In the 1940s and 50s, though, Mongita was better known as an artist. Largely self-taught, Mongita initially sold his paintings door-to-door but he later studied under both Laurent Moones at the Stanley Park school and with the Brazilian artist Guilherme Marques. He specialized in landscape painting and created a simple and balanced colour palette. He also painted dance and hunting scenes and other aspects of "traditional" life. Albert Mongita’s reputation was secured when in 1947 he received his first solo exhibition in Leopoldville and his painting ‘Kalina Point’ was given to the Belgian Prince Regent. He then exhibited in the 1948 Antwerp Colonial Exhibition in Belgium. The Smithsonian holds a paper on him written by Andre Scohy in 1948 titled ‘Albert Mongita, les autodidactes’. A Mongita painting was also given by Isaac Kalonji Mutambayi (a future President of the National Senate) to USA President Dwight D. Eisenhower in 1957.
