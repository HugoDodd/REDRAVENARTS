Alphonse Kiabelua

1927–1980s

Artist Biography

Alphonse Kiabelua was a pioneer Congolese artist from the Democratic Republic of Congo (known as Belgian Congo before independence in 1960). He was one of a small group of self-taught indigenous artists, including Albert Dombe, who around 1947 gathered at the Ecole de Stanley Pool in Leopoldville (lKinshasa). The Belgian painter Laurent Moonens won a travel scholarship to Congo in 1948 and saw the work of Kiabelua and the other Leopoldville artists but Moonens felt that already the naive-style work was too contaminated by Western ideas. Kiabelua was one of just five artists to form the subject of an article by Andre Scohy in 1951 called ‘Congolese Painting: Today’s Problems’ and also a film by Scohy in 1952 called ‘Bantu Painters’.

One source claims he lived in Paris but frequently visited Congo. There is little biographical information on Kiabelua but the Smithsonian Library in the United States has some papers on him and the University of Ghent in Belgium has a pamphlet titled ‘Aux Congo Belge: Portrait Alphonse Kiabelua’. His work has been included in a number of major international exhibitions on African and Congolese art, including a1979 exhibition in Berlin called Modern Art from Africa, a 2015 exhibition of Congolese Painters from 1920-2015 at the Cartier Foundation in Paris and in in 2016/17another in the Palais des Beaux Arts in Brussels entitled Congolese Art.
