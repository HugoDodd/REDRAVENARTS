Alphonse Moto

B. 1938

Artist Biography

Alphonse Moto was born in Buta, in the north of the then Belgian Congo (now Democratic Republic of the Congo). He studied art in Brazzaville, most likely largely under European teachers, before spending two years in Europe furthering his studies and supporting himself through the sale of his work. He held two successful shows in Brussels, followed by further exhibitions in France and Germany. Upon returning to the Congo, political unrest forced him to relocate to Uganda. Disillusioned with the art scene in the Congo, Moto noted in an August 1962 interview with the Belgian newspaper Le Soir that even formally trained artists had to seek white patrons due to the lack of local resources for art patronage.

By the mid-1960s, Moto had moved from Uganda to settle in Nairobi, Kenya. While finding success there, he often felt compelled to paint wildlife scenes to cater to a primarily European and colonial clientele. Moto was one of a select group of African artists of his era to achieve international exhibition success. Highlights include a major solo show at the Uganda Gallery in Kampala in 1966, an exhibition at Gallery Africa in Nairobi in 1970 alongside Eli Kyeyune, Francis Msangi, and Edward Njenga, and a solo show in Tucson, Arizona in 1971.
