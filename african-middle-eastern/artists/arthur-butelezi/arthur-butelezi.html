Arthur Butelezi

fl.1930s – 1940s

Artist Biography

Arthur Butelezi is one of three artists, the others being Gerard Beghu and Simon Mnguni, who are considered to be the pioneers of black African art in KwaZulu-Natal. None were formally trained. Butelezi, who was based in Rorke’s Drift, was an important social realist painter whose strong and striking portraits are said to reveal much of the sitter’s identity and character partly because they were almost certainly of people he knew. Most of his work is of portraits in watercolour but in later years he turned to oils too with impressive results. Butelezi’s paintings are in the Campbell-Smith collection of South African art, probably the finest collection of works by black South African painters. As such a number were displayed in the Revisions exhibition held in 2005-2006 at the South African National Gallery in Cape Town. Butelezi painted one of the murals for the Wesleyan Methodist Institute in Durban 1942-43 (Benghu completed the rest) but his work seems mainly to have been bought privately by white South Africans.
