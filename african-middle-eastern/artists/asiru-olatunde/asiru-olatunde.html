Asiru Olatunde

1919–1992

Artist Biography

Asiru Olatunde came from a family of traditional blacksmiths in Oshogbo, Nigeria but illness meant he was unable to continue working in the profession and so he turned to making earrings and other trinkets from copper. Ulli Beier first met Olatunde in 1958 and in 1961 he persuaded him to undertake larger scale work, often on 25cm copper panels depicting stories from Osogbo culture. Olatunde’s work became known far beyond Oshogbo and the University of Ibadan and a breakthrough came when a major hotel in Lagos commissioned two large panels. These were more practical in aluminium and after this Olatunde largely used that metal for his repousse metalwork panels. Olatunde is by far the best known – and arguably the most talented - of the Oshogbo metal panel sculptors. His work was included from 1965 in many international exhibitions of Oshogbo work, including in Germany, Austria, UK and the USA. Solo exhibitions included a posthumous retrospective in 1992 at Iwalewa-Haus, Bayreuth, Germany and ‘Chasing Dreams’ at John Martin Gallery, London in 2005. His work was also shown at Nigerian Modernism at the Tate Modern in London 2025-26.
