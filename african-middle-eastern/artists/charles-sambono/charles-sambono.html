Charles Sambono

1954–2021

Artist Biography

Charles Sambono was a Zambian-born artist who worked primarily in oil and pastels and enjoyed international acclaim. He held his first exhibition at age 14 and went on to present some 60 shows across four continents, exhibiting in Zambia, Nigeria, Japan, England, the USA, Germany, and Switzerland, among other locations. He relocated to London in the 1970s, where his work was exhibited at several notable venues, including the groundbreaking Westbourne Gallery (later Karnak House), which focused on contemporary work by African and Caribbean diaspora artists.

In London, Sambono's work was included in the major group exhibition The Colours of Black at the Greater London Council and a solo exhibition in 1987 at the Commonwealth Institute. He spent his final two decades in Switzerland, where he was represented by Gallery Helvetica from 2001. In 2006, he won first prize at the Salon d'Automne International in Marseilles.

In his vibrant paintings, Sambono explored themes of identity, gender, and the power and vulnerability of human relationships, emphasizing them through sweeping lines, curves, and bold colors. Using just a few expressive lines with distinct virtuosity, his compositions captured the sensuality of human interaction. One of Sambono's paintings was selected for the cover of Ifi Amadiume's 1987 book African Matriarchal Foundations.
