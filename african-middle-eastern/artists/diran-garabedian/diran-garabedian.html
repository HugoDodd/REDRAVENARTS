Diran Garabedian

1882–1963

Artist Biography

Diran Garabedian was born in Cairo of mixed Armenian and Egyptian heritage. From 1898-1900 he studied at the Khorenian Armenian School in Egypt under the guidance of Ervand Demirdjian. In 1900, like Demirdjian before him, Garabedian entered L'Academie Julien in Paris; Garabedian studied there for four years, notably under Benjamin Constant and Jean-Paul Laurens, and in 1905 received the degree of L'Ecole des Beau Arts. It was in this year that he decided to break away from the traditional style in favour of an expressionist modernism, becoming one of Egypt’s first avant-garde artists. In many of Garabedian's works there can be seen his passion for fish and birds, which became one of his trademarks. Garabedian painted portraits, landscapes and still lives but these often verged on the surreal and abstract. He exhibited alongside Braque, Fugita, Utrillo, Picasso, Chagall and Cocteau amongst others at the Galerie Carmine, Paris in 1928. Garabedian spent his Summers in France, particularly Brittany, returning to Egypt in the Winter months. His oils were often sombre, his watercolours vibrant. Many of his paintings, which he frequently failed to sign, remained in his family’s hands and have only been released on to the market in recent years. Garabedian was one of the artists whose work was chosen for a major exhibition in Cairo in 2009 celebrating artists of Armenian heritage in Egypt. He remains, though little known, one of Egypt’s first genuine modernists.
