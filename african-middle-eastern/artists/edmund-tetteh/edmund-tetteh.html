Edmund Tetteh

1924–2017

Artist Biography

Most art databases give Tetteh’s dates as 1938-1998, but the life dates given above are from a major retrospective held in Accra in 2019 and are thus more likely to be accurate. Tetteh was taught at the Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology (KNUST) in Kumasi by James Hillocks, a Scottish artist. Hillocks taught there from 1952 and the period from then until 1962 is known as ‘the Scottish period’ in Ghanaian art. Tetteh went on to teach at the same university and, whilst a senior lecturer there, was made a visiting Fulbright scholar at the University of Pennsylvania 1974-5. Tetteh went on to become the Professor at the Department of Paintings and Sculptures at KNUST. As such he was a member of the Arts Council of the African Studies Association. Tetteh kept his links with Scotland and had an exhibition of his watercolours in 1989 at the Interdec Gallery in Glasgow. True to his education, Tetteh was known as a fine colourist and although he was considered a painter of Kumasi Realism many of his works verged on the abstract. The 2019 exhibition was at the Dei Centre for the Study of Contemporary Art in Accra and featured Tetteh’s work alongside that of Albert Osabu Bartimeus and Owusu Dartey, the three being described as the ‘undisputed fathers of Ghanaian modern art’ and Tetteh as a ‘master colourist’.
