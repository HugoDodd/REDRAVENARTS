Emmanuel Taiwo Jegede

B. 1943

Artist Biography

Emmanuel Taiwo Jegede was born in Ekit, a Yoruba-speaking part of Nigeria. He studied sculpture at the Yaba School of Technology in Lagos before in 1963 travelling to London where he attended Hammersmith School of Art. A poet and an artist, known for his painting, prints and sculpture, Jegede developed a style which drew inspiration from Yoruba art but was also influenced by European modernism. His first exhibition was at Woodstock Gallery, London in 1968. He moved to studios at Riverside, Hammersmith in 1970 and in the following decade he developed a reputation as an art activist and advocate for the Black British artistic community. He took part in many exhibitions and in 1977 was among the Black British artists representing the UK at FESTEC ’77 in Lagos, Nigeria. In 1978 Jegede was a founding member, alongside Ugo Ezonu and Lancelot Ribeiro, of the Rainbow Art Group. Notable exhibitions which included his work were ‘Transforming the Crown: African and Caribbean Artists in Britain 1966-1996’ shown at the Caribbean Cultural Centre in New York 1997/98 and 2015’s ‘No Colour Bar: Black British Art in Action 1960-1990’ at the Guildhall Art Gallery, City of London. In 2011 Jegede received a Special Recognition Award from the US House of Representatives for contributions to African culture. His work is held in public collections in Nigeria, Trinidad, Jamaica and the UK.
