Eve de Negri

1919–1977

Artist Biography

Eve de Negri was born in Britain of Italian extraction. She combined painting with lecturing at Central St Martin’s School of Design in London before emigrating to Ibadan in Nigeria’s Western Region in 1959, remaining in Nigeria for the rest of her life. She extensively researched local clothing, costumes, and body adornment, authoring foundational books and articles throughout the 1960s and 1970s that remain key references on West African material culture.

After moving to Lagos in the mid-1960s, de Negri worked alongside pioneer modern artist Yusuf Grillo to establish the Fashion Department at the Yaba College of Technology. Grillo described her as "a very good painter from Britain [with] a flair for fashion design." Her artistic production closely mirrored her ethnographical studies, capturing human presence, dress, and daily life in Nigeria with expressive painterly form.

De Negri exhibited at the Royal Academy in London in 1963, and in 1965 became the first woman to hold a solo exhibition at the Commonwealth Institute in London, titled Paintings of Nigeria (documented in a British Pathé newsreel). She held major solo exhibitions in Lagos at the Exhibition Centre in Marina (1965) and the Independence Building (1966). Her drawings were featured in the 1969 UNICEF calendar, and her work is represented in the permanent collection of the Yusuf Grillo Museum in Lagos.
