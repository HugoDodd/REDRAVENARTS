Faustin Kitsiba

c.1920s – 1990s

Artist Biography

Faustin Kitsiba was born in Boko in the then French Congo, now Republic of Congo. Largely self-taught as an artist, Kitsiba moved to the capital Brazzaville and by the 1940s is thought to have had his own atelier. His path crossed with those of two other pioneer artists there, Gaspard de Mouko and Guy-Leon Fylla, all earning a living largely through portraiture. The art scene in Brazzaville took off in the early 1950s when Pierre Lods set up an art class that became known as the Poto-Poto school. Kitsiba had links with Poto-Poto, but having started earlier and then later developed an independent artistic style these should not be exaggerated. Kitsiba’s paintings, which included modernist and expressionist works in addition to more realistic fare, remained popular for many years. He took part in major group exhibitions in Brazzaville in 1963 and 1969, in the latter case providing the majority of the works selected. He is believed to have had paintings exhibited across Central Africa; his paintings were also shown at the 1966 First World Festival of Black Arts in Dakar and in a mixed exhibition in Paris. Kitsiba also tutored upcoming talent; his best-known pupils included Cameroonian painters Koko Komegne and Pascal Kenfack. Now recognised as a doyenne of the Congolese school of art, work from 1991 indicates Kitsiba enjoyed a long career despite having by then long faded from the spotlight.
