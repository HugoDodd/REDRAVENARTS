Gaspard de Mouko

c.1915–c.1970s

Artist Biography

Gaspard de Mouko was born in Douala, Cameroon and was first noted as painting in the late 1930s. He moved to Brazzaville, capital of the then French Congo, in the early 1940s and became a key figure in the growth of modern art in the city. He made a living as a professional painter, selling his work and training local artists such as Eugene Malonga and Faustin Kitsiba and in effect becoming a founder of what later became the Poto-Poto school of art. Noted for his landscapes and portraits – including of both General de Gaulle and first deputy Jean-Felix Tchikaya, a major political figure in pre-independence Congo – de Mouko relocated to Bangui in the Central African Republic in 1950 and then later to Abidjan in Ivory Coast. A very important pioneer in early modern art in Francophone Central Africa, de Mouko was self-trained. This might help explain why he has not had the prominence of similar pioneers in the English-speaking African world, such as Ben Enwonwu and Gerard Sekoto, but his work was ‘Africanist’, powerful and remains an important marker in the development of modern African painting.
