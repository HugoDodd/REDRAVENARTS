Georgina Beier

1938–2021

Artist Biography

Georgina Beier (née Betts) was born in London in 1938. At sixteen, she enrolled at Kingston Art School before leaving formal study and moving to Nigeria in 1959, where she met and married Ulli Beier. In 1964, Beier began running seminal art workshops at the Mbari Mbayo Club in Osogbo, Nigeria. Rejecting academic constraints, she encouraged innate creative authenticity, fostering pioneer Osogbo artists such as Twins Seven Seven, Muraina Oyelami, and Rufus Ogundele.

Beier was intimately involved in the theatre company at Mbari Mbayo, designing stage backdrops, costumes, and religious ceremonial accoutrements. Her paintings and prints from this period drew heavily on Yoruba iconography, mythology, and visual symbolism.

After leaving Nigeria in 1966, Beier lived and worked in Papua New Guinea, Australia, India, and Germany, continually conducting workshops with local artists while sustaining a diverse personal practice across painting, sculpture, textiles, lithographs, furniture, and book illustration. Her work was showcased in Tate Modern’s landmark Century City: Lagos exhibition (2001) and featured in Nigerian Modernism at Tate Modern, London (2025–26).
