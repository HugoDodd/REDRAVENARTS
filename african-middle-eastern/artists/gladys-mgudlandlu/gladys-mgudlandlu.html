Gladys Mgudlandlu

1923–1979

Artist Biography

Mgudlandlu was born near Peddie in the Eastern Cape c.1923 into a Mfengu family, and moved to Cape Town in 1944 where she remained until her death in 1979. Mgudlandlu was a visionary artist whose style and subject-matter stand apart from those of her contemporaries. Her work was not rooted in the socio-political realism that characterised the work of most black artists of the time, or the formalism of the white artists of the period.

She achieved fame in the early 1960s, with initial solo shows at the Liberal party offices in 1961 and Rodin Gallery in 1962. The artist said at the time, ‘I think I can claim to be the first African woman in the country to hold an exhibition. As far as I know, I am the only African woman who has taken painting seriously. It has become my first love and there is nothing else I want to do’ (Cape Argus, 15.8.1962). She exhibited with success for the next decade until she was seriously injured in a motor car accident in 1971 but due recognition of her artistry was only accorded to her in the early 2000s. A biography by Elza Miles: Nomfanekiso: who paints at night. The art of Gladys Mgudlandlu, Cape Town, was published in 2003 and in 2012 an exhibition of her works was held in the Johannesburg Art Gallery. A Mgudlandlu painting was shown in the South African pavilion at the Venice Biennale in 2024 and another is in the Nelson Mandela Metropolitan Museum in Port Elizabeth.
