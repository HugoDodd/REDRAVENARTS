Hussein Shariffe

1934–2005

Artist Biography

Hussein Shariffe, a Sudanese poet, painter and filmmaker, was born in Omdurman, Sudan to an elite family (a great grandson of Ahmed Al-Mahdi, the ‘Mad Mahdi’). He studied history at Cambridge, art at the Slade School in London and finally film at the National Film School UK. Painting was his first love and through the 1960s and early 1970s Shariffe tried to inspire a Sudanese following for his art, before turning to film so that he could reach a wider audience. He had two spells teaching at The School of Fine Arts, Khartoum. His first solo exhibition was at Gallery One in 1960, followed by further shows at the gallery in 1962 and 1963. After his return to Sudan in 1964 he had a number of solo shows there, a 1971 exhibition in Gallery One in Beirut, Lebanon, and many in Egypt where he lived through the 1990s. He also had solo exhibitions in Germany in 1996 and 1998. Amongst the more interesting group shows were the 1963 John Moore Biennale at Walker Art Gallery, Liverpool (where he won the first prize for painting in the junior section), the 1967 9th Biennale in Sao Paolo in Brazil, the 1986 Arab Contemporary Art show at the Mall Galleries and 1998 exhibition at Orlando Museum of Art, Florida. The Al-Ahram Weekly in 2003, in an in-depth article, described Shariffe as “perhaps Sudan’s foremost abstract painter”. His work is also in the Barjeel Collection and was displayed at Christies London in 2023 as part of the ‘Modern and Contemporary Art in the Arab World’ exhibition organised by the Barjeel Art Foundation.
