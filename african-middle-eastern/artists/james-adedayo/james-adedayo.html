James Adedayo

B. 1941

Artist Biography

James Ishola Adedayo was born on January 1, 1941, in Ila-Orangun, Nigeria. He gained his artistic training at the celebrated Osogbo art workshops from 1966 to 1968, followed by study at the Ori-Olukun Cultural Centre at the University of Ife from 1969 to 1972. At Ife, Adedayo experimented with diverse printing and textile techniques, including metal embossing, etching, wax, and batik.

Adedayo held notable solo exhibitions at the Ori-Olukun Cultural Centre and in Nairobi, Kenya (1972), Budapest, Hungary (1973), and the USIS in Lagos (1977). A major 1977 exhibition at Sydney University featured 97 works alongside live singing, miming, and dance performances by Adedayo acting out the themes of his paintings. His group exhibitions in the 1970s spanned Nigeria, the Maxwell Art Centre in London, the Crocker Art Gallery in Sacramento, and later the French Cultural Centres in Lome and Cotonou, as well as Atlanta, Georgia.

He founded the Osogbo Afro Art and Research Centre in Osun State to foster research and collaboration among local artists. His work is held in numerous public collections in Nigeria, as well as the University of Ghana in Legon, Iwalewa-Haus at the University of Bayreuth in Germany, and Spelman College in Atlanta. Adedayo’s work was also included in the Robin Williams collection, forming part of a touring Osogbo art exhibition in the United States that featured at the Waldron Arts Center in Indianapolis in 2024.
