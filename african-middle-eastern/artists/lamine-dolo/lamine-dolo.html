Lamine Dolo

fl. 1960s/70s/80s

Artist Biography

Lamine Dolo was an artist from the Dogon region who came to prominence in the 1960s and was one of Mali’s most renowned artists through the 1970s and 1980s. A New York Times interview in 1972 described him as a ‘leading Dogon artist’ who lives and teaches art in Bamoko but who alsoreturned to his ancestral village of Sangha for a couple of months each year. In 2010 Dolo was one of ten artists nominated by the culture ministry from the period 1960-70 in celebration of fifty years of independence. Whist the local art scene is now much more vibrant, leading Malian artist Abdoulaye Konate interviewed in 2020 lamented that “when Lamine Dolo exhibited in the 1970s and 1980s, it was generally foreigners who came to see exhibitions”. Dolo also exhibited abroad, including in Canada in 1975. In addition he was an important player in early Malian cinema; he was the set designer for Souleiman Cisse’s 1975 debut film The Young Girl and a number of other films and acted in the 1978 Sega Coulibaly film The Prisoner. Dolo was one of the 132 artists who contributed to Ted Joans’ nine metre long work Long Distance which was hung in 2022 at the Tate Modern, UK and the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York as part of the Surrealism Beyond Borders exhibition.
