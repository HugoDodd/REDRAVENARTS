Larry Scully

1922–2002

Artist Biography

Larry Scully was born in Gibraltar to a South African mother and the family moved when he was fifteen to Johannesburg. Scully trained as a draughtsman but in the late 1940s taught art at the Polly Street Arts Centre before studying from 1947-50 at the University of Witwatersrand. There he met fellow students such as Cecil Skotnes and Christo Coetzee. He taught art at Pretoria Boys High School for many years before becoming a Professor of Fine Art at the University of Stellenbosch, the town he made his home. Scully represented South Africa at the Venice and Sao Paolo biennales in the 1960s and in 1973 was commissioned to create his most famous work ‘Madonna and Child in Soweto’ which hangs in the Regina Mundi church there. Scully used a wide variety of media and experimented in both allegory and abstraction. A major retrospective in 2002 at the Sasol Art Museum in Stellenbosch was underway when Scully died.
