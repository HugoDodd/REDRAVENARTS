Louis Mwaniki

1934–2013

Artist Biography

Louis Mwaniki was born in Nyeri, Kenya and educated at Catholic schools there before becoming only the third Kenyan to attend Makerere University’s Fine Art Department in Kampala, Uganda. This institution, founded by Margaret Trowell, led art education in East Africa in the 1950s, and Mwaniki studied there from 1957 to 1961, sometimes assisting in teaching. At this time, he produced largely naturalistic works, many with a distinct political stance—notably his Emergency series documenting atrocities in Kenya during the Mau-Mau rebellion.

In 1961, Mwaniki left to study in Turin, Italy, where he stayed until 1963. After a period back in Kenya teaching art at Nairobi University, he traveled to the United States and Canada in the late 1960s, finally settling back in Kenya where he taught at Nairobi’s Kenyatta University from 1972 to 2010.

Mwaniki illustrated a number of books in the 1960s and became a fine printmaker and sculptor as well as a painter. His style became more abstract in later years. As one of Kenya’s first-generation modern painters, Mwaniki has been called "Kenya’s Picasso" and was described by Terry Hirst (founder of Kenyatta University’s Fine Art Department in 1966) as "an outstanding Kenyan artist of international repute." His major commissions include a large mural at Kerugoya Catholic Church, which was the subject of a documentary on Italian television, and sculptural works held in public collections in Slovenia and the UK. He had a solo exhibition at the Paa ya Paa (PAP) Art Centre in Nairobi in 1966 and was honored with a major retrospective there in 2013 following his passing.
