Michael O'Rorke

1895–1981

Artist Biography

Michael O’Rorke was born in Ireland and flew as a fighter pilot in the First World War before joining the newly formed Palestine Police in 1922. A talented artist, he illustrated a book by General Wavell about General Allenby published in 1940. During World War II, O’Rorke was in charge of policing occupied areas in the Middle East and North East Africa, later serving as a military policeman in Germany until 1950, when he became Police Commissioner in Kenya during the height of the Mau Mau rebellion.

Described by one colonial officer as "a very unusual Commissioner... who looked like a down-at-heel jockey with unfathomably long hair," O’Rorke retired early in 1954 after clashing with colonial officials. He remained in Kenya and, after a year studying at art school in Nairobi, dedicated himself to painting portraits of local people and landscapes, mainly in oils. His works captured Kenyan life during the pre- and early independence eras. O’Rorke held a major solo exhibition at the New Stanley Gallery in Nairobi in 1963. Following independence, he left Kenya for Europe. A monograph, Michael O’Rorke: Policeman Artist, was published in 2006.
