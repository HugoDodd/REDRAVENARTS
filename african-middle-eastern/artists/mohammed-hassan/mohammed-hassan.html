Mohammed Hassan

1892–1961

Artist Biography

Mohammed Hassan was born in Al-Daqahalia, Egypt. He joined the Cairo School of Fine Arts when it opened in 1908, the first fine art school in the Arab world, and quickly became noted as a talented artist. By 1910 he had been chosen, a year before graduation, to teach at the newly set up department of decoration at the college. He taught there for seven years before a two-year stint from 1917-19 at the London Central School of Arts and Crafts. Here he studied painting, sculpture casting and other skills. He returned to Cairo to teach at the School of Egyptian Arts and Decoration, mainly in the applied arts. A further government scholarship took him to Rome, Italy in 1925 where – along with Yusuf Kamal and Ragheb Aiad – he gained a certificate in fine art. He stayed in Italy, honing his painting skills, notably in portraiture and classical subjects but also in caricature, before returning to Egypt in 1929 as Deputy Head at the Applied Arts School. He was one of the first Egyptians to take up a senior position in the arts and became the school’s principal in 1937. Hassan was a leader of the modern art revival in Egypt, a key first generation artist. He was a fine portrait artist but also gained prominence in the field of sculpture. A group of his sculptures, in a classical style, adorn the gardens at Cairo Opera House. Even so, his teaching and other duties meant he was not a prolific artist and so his surviving artworks are far from numerous. One of the better known is a portrait of King Farouk in the Heritage Collection Museum in Dokki, Giza. Works by Hassan also adorned the collection of the Museum of Modern Art in Cairo when it first opened in 1928 and remain in it now.
