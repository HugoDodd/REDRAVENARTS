Oluwola Olayemi

B. 1947

Artist Biography

Oluwola Olayemi was born in Ghana to Yoruba migrant parents but returned to the Osogbo region in his youth and, on finishing education, went to work in Ife as a clerk. He had his first artistic training at the 1969-70 Ori Olokun workshops led by Solomon Wangboje at the University of Ife. Olayemi later studied under Yusuf Grillo at the Yaba College of Technology in Lagos. Although he is considered to be from the Ife-Ife school, Olayemi remained close to his Osogbo roots and his work, mainly linocuts, often depicted subjects drawn from Yoruba culture. In Nigeria he held exhibitions at USIS Ibadan and ABU in Zaria and at the British Council in Ibadan in 1975. By that stage he had also had solo exhibitions in Los Angeles and Cleveland State University, Ohio in the United States. Olayemi’s work has been shown in major international mixed exhibitions; he was one of the artists shown in an exhibition on Osogbo art at the Museum of African Art in Belgrade in 1988. His prints were also shown in 2020 at Kruizenga Art Museum Hope College, Michigan in the exhibition ‘Resilience, Resistance and Revival in 20th century Yoruba Art’ and also at the Smithsonian’s National Museum of African Art in Washington from 2024-2025 in ‘Bruce Onobrakpeya: The Mask and the Cross’ when he was one of five contemporaries whose work was juxtaposed with that of Onobrakpeya. Collections permanently holding Olayemi’s work include the Fonb Collection in Maryland, LeHigh University, Pennsylvania and Hope College, Michigan.
