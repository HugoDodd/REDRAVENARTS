Rufus Ogundele

1946–1996

Artist Biography

Rufus Ogundele was born in Oshogbo, Nigeria. He received training in the arts under Denis Williams and then Georgina Beier in 1963 and 1964, then in 1965 attended a printing workshop under Ru Van Rossem. Ogundele’s work, bold in conception and often in size, married this Western training with traditional Yoruba art. He was one of the most highly regarded of what became known as the first generation Oshogbo artists, one of the key Nigerian art movements. In 1968 he moved to Ife, where he set up the Ogun-Timehin studios. He remained there - other than for a year’s artist residence in Bayreuth, Germany in 1983 - until his early death.

Ogundele’s work was exhibited widely, including regular solo shows at the Goethe Institute, Lagos in the 1970s. Mixed exhibitions including his work visited New York, Washington, Berlin, London (Contemporary Art from Africa at the ICA in 1967), Prague, Senegal and elsewhere with the highlight perhaps the show of Oshogbo art from the 1960s at the National Museum of African Art (the Smithsonian Museum) in Washington in 2000. Ogundele’s prints were also exhibited at the National Museum of African Art in Washington from 2024-205, in ‘Bruce Onobrakpeya: The Mask and the Cross’ when he was one of five contemporaries whose work was juxtaposed with that of Onobrakpeya. In addition Ogundele’s paintings and prints were included in the major exhibition African Modernism in America 1947-67, held in 2023-24 at the Phillips Collection in Washington, United States and at Nigerian Modernism at the Tate Modern in London 2025-26. Ogundele’s works are in public collections in the US, Germany and the National Gallery of Modern Art in Lagos.
