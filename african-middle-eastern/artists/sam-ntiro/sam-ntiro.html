Sam Ntiro

1923–1993

Artist Biography

Sam Joseph Ntiro was born near Mount Kilimanjaro, Tanzania. He studied under Margaret Trowell at Makerere University College School of Fine Art in Kampala, Uganda, from 1944 to 1947. He subsequently attended the Slade School of Fine Art in London starting in 1952. By the time he was awarded a Carnegie Scholarship to the United States in 1960, he was considered East Africa’s best-known painter. While in New York, he held a solo exhibition at the Merton Simpson Gallery, and the Museum of Modern Art (MoMA) acquired one of his works for its permanent collection. Ntiro was widely regarded as the first East African artist to exhibit in New York and the first African artist on Madison Avenue. He also served as artist-in-residence at Southern University, New Orleans.

From 1961 to 1964, Ntiro served as Tanzania’s High Commissioner in London before returning to academia as a professor at Makerere University. Recognized as Tanzania’s most famous painter, his stylized works—often depicting the daily life and landscape of the Chagga people—are instantly recognizable. In addition to MoMA in New York and the National Gallery of Art in Washington, D.C., his paintings are held in public collections including the UK Government Art Collection, the British Empire and Commonwealth Museum in Bristol, the Imatra Art Museum in Finland, and the Bridgeman Art Library. His work was also featured in the major touring exhibition African Modernism in America 1947–67 (2023–2024), organized by the Phillips Collection in Washington, D.C.
