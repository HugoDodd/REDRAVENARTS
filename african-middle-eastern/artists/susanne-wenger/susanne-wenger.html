Susanne Wenger

1915–2009

Artist Biography

Susanne Wenger was born in Graz, Austria and studied at the Academy of Fine Arts in Vienna. Her art tended towards surrealism and spirituality and, after surviving the horrors of war, in 1946 she became a founding member of the Vienna Art Club. In 1949 she met and married the linguist Ulli Beier and in 1950 the couple arrived in Ibadan, Nigeria where Beier had been offered a lecture post. Beier became interested in the local Yoruba culture in its many forms, particularly after the couple moved to Osogbo in 1958, becoming an important supporter of its resurgence in the 1960s. Wenger though immersed herself in the local Orisha religion and, over time, divorced Beier and married a Yoruba drummer. Wenger created art using traditional batik printing methods before inventing her own method of textile painting, wax batik and indigo dye. Her subjects – in a very distinct semi-surreal style – largely revolved around the Yoruba gods but she became best known for her work on restoring and recreating, with the help of other Osogbo artists, the Sacred Grove of Osogbo. This grove of shrines to the various Orisha gods was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2006. Wenger, who had Nigerian citizenship from 1959 until her death, was always well known in Nigeria but has become recognised increasingly in Europe for her work, having a first major exhibition in Vienna in 1985 and then many others elsewhere since. Her work was also shown at Nigerian Modernism at the Tate Modern in London 2025-26.
