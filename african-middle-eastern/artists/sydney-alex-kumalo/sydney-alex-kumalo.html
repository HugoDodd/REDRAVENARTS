Sydney Alex Kumalo

1935–1988

Artist Biography

Stanley Kumalo was born in Sophiatown, Johannesburg in South Africa and was brought up in Soweto. From 1952-57 he attended the Polly Street Art Centre, under the guidance of Cecil Skotnes for whom he worked as an assistant. Initially he painted in watercolours but he then trained under sculptor Edoardo Villa, mostly in metal castings. Kumalo taught at Polly Street from 1960-64, resigning to become a full-time artist. His international career took off in the mid-1960s, participating in mixed exhibitions in Britain, Europe the United States and elsewhere. Highlights included the 1967 Sao Paolo biennial in Brazil and in 1969 when he represented South Africa in the Contemporary African Art Exhibition in Camden, London. Kumalo’s sculptures often merged the human and the animal, in styles drawing from early carvings and tribal art but with strong modernist and expressionist influences absorbed from Polly Street Art Centre tutors. Although best known for sculpture, Kumalo continued to paint in pastels and watercolours throughout. His work was included in the 1981 exhibition Black Art Today in Soweto and in 2023 Strauss & Co in Johannesburg gave him and Ezrom Legae a major retrospective.
