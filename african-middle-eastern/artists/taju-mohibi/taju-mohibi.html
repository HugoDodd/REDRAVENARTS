Taju Mohibi

1951–2001

Artist Biography

Taju Akanbi Mohibi was born in Iseyin, Oyo State in Nigeria. He trained in 1970 in Tunde Allen-Taylor’s studio before studying fine arts at the University of Ibadan in 1972. The period from 1974 saw Mohibi practicing as an artist based in Ife whilst also carrying out workshops in various Nigerian colleges and art institutions. Mohibi was a polymath and was a musician (flautist and drummer), dancer, actor, playwright and storyteller in addition to his painting, batik making and basket weaving. His paintings were noted for a bold use of image and colour and, whilst clearly based on Yoruba culture, veered towards the modernist and abstract. In Nigeria Mohibi was known particularly for his murals and he received major commissions for these in Abeokuta, Ibadan, Lagos and Kano. He moved to the United States in 1986, initially as artist-in-residence at Shaka International Art Shrine in Baltimore but spent the later 1980s and early 1990s teaching various arts in a number of US institutions. A member of the Society of Nigerian Artists, Mohibi had numerous solos shows including at the University of Ibadan in 1972 and 1973, the British Council in Ibadan 1976, the Village Gates Pavillion in Lagos 1978 and others in Lagos in 1981 and 1982. Solo exhibitions abroad included two in Hungary in 1984 and 1985 and then from 1987 a string in the United States in cities such as Detroit, Baltimore and Passadena in California. Highlights of group exhibitions were shows in East Berlin in 1973, Hamburg 1976 and at the National Art Museum in Lagos in 1981 plus many in the United States in the 1980s and 1990s. He was also one of the artists shown in an exhibition on Osogbo art at the Museum of African Art in Belgrade in 1988.
