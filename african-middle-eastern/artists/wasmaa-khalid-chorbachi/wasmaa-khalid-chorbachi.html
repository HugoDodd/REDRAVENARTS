Wasma’a Khalid Chorbachi

B. 1944

Artist Biography

Wasma’a Chorbachi was born in Cairo to Iraqi parents but was brought up in Baghdad. She attended Beirut College, Lebanon in 1966 and then studied art in Florence, Italy in 1967 before in the late 1980s undertaking a PhD at Harvard in the United States. She has spent much of her adult life in the United States but her art, as a ceramicist, calligrapher and a painter, reflects deeply on her Middle Eastern and Islamic heritage. Chorbachi is also a noted academic art historian with an interest in mathematics.

After a period painting in Western abstract styles Chorbachi felt this did not adequately express her heritage and inner feelings and from the late 1960s she turned towards calligraphic and traditional Arabic patterns. Chorbachi’s work, mainly in ceramics, is held in a number of public collections including the Museum of Fine Arts in Boston and Harvard University in the United States, the British Museum in London and Jordan’s Modern Gallery of Fine Arts. Solo exhibitions have been held in Beirut, 1966, 68, and 70; Florence, 1967; Abu Dhabi, 1976; Jedda, 1981; Cambridge, Massachusetts, 1983; London, 1984 and 85; Al-Khubar, Saudi Arabia, 1990 and the Sackler Gallery, Washington in 2001. She participated in the group exhibition Forces of change presented in 1994 at the National Museum of Women Artists in Washington, where her work was described as "abstract expressionist" and in a 2008 exhibition at the British Museum, London Word into Art: Artists of the Modern Middle East. Her work is also in the Barjeel Collection and was displayed at Christies London in 2023 as part of the ‘Modern and Contemporary Art in the Arab World’ exhibition organised by the Barjeel Art Foundation.
