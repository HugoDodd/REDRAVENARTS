Chien-Ying Chang

1913-2003

Artist Biography

Chien-Ying Chang studied art at the Central University of Nanking 1931-35, where she met her future husband Chung-Wei Fei. Influenced by Xu Beihong, with whom she helped set up the China Institute of Fine Arts in Chongking, and his belief that Chinese artists should also be exposed to Western artistic ideas, Chang and Wei travelled to Britain in 1946 on British Council scholarships. Both studied at the Slade School of Art in London and, after the Communist revolution altered their plans to return to China, went on to forge successful careers in Britain.

The English painter Stanley Spencer became a friend and painted three portraits of Chang. Chang showed at the Royal Academy and at the Society of Woman Artists and Royal Society of Painters in Watercolours. She also had a series of solo shows at the Leicester Galleries in Leicester Square, London in the late 1950s and 1960s. She made fabric designs for Christian Dior and her prints became enormously popular in Britain but Chien-Ying Chang’s legacy lies in her ink, wash and watercolour painting in a spare classical Chinese style, employing lots of space, but married to Western sensibilities. The British Government Art Collection and Graves Art Gallery, Sheffield hold her work.
