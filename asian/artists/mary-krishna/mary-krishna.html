Mary Krishna

1909–1968

Artist Biography

Mary Krishna was born Mary Oldfield and studied at the Royal College of Art in London from 1928-1930, where she met Indian artist Roop Krishna. Roop returned to his home city of Lahore (then India, now Pakistan) in 1930 before Mary followed two years later. When the couple married in 1936 Mary took the Indian name Indumati Roop Krishna, which she used when signing many of her works in this period. Mary and Roop studied under Abanindranath Tagore, met other artists based around the Lahore School of Fine Arts and later taught classes there. Mary, like Roop, worked in a style that combined Western artistic influences with Indian techniques and subject matter; her work ranged from dark expressionist oils portraying street scenes and figures, through more abstracted works to bright watercolours depicting masks and gods. A study on silk by Mary was awarded the Maharaja of Baroda’s prize in 1937 and she also completed large-scale murals on the themes of Indian industry and agriculture at the since demolished Imperial Institute in London. Mary and Roop shared a joint exhibition at the Punjab Literary Leage in Lahore in 1938 and they co-authored in 1940 and 1943 two volumes, ‘Art and Life’ and ‘Echo and Ego’, that were influential arguments for a universal artistic sensibility and for the need for Indian painters to pursue artistic routes that could break with earlier traditions. The couple, hoping for more exposure for their art, returned permanently to London in 1946, and between 1955 and 1967 Mary exhibited regularly at the Royal Academy. She was also an Associate member of the Royal College of Art. She had work included in mixed exhibitions at Leicester Galleries and elsewhere in London. In her later years Mary Krishna became best known for free flowing and abstracted depictions of ballet dancers and circus acts. Belgrave Gallery in London gave her a retrospective in 1978.
