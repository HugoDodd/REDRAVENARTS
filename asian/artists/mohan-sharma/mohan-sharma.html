Mohan Sharma

1942–1988

Artist Biography

Born into a family of Nathdwara miniaturist painters, Sharma graduated from the Sir J.J. School of Art in Bombay in 1965. His work caught the eye of F.N. Souza who was looking to experiment and change his own style. Souza invited Sharma to his London studio and the pair collaborated on a series of paintings marrying their styles. These were exhibited at the Taj Art Gallery in Bombay in 1965. However, whilst at the JJ School of Art Sharma had edited a book titled ‘Cubism’ by artist and critic Sambhaji Kadam and it was in a cubist/abstract direction that Sharma took his work. There was a spareness and beauty in Sharma’s paintings of cities which represented a vision of divinity but also loneliness within the most human of environments. Souza wrote that in Mohan Sharma’s art "there is a remarkable understanding of cosmological structures.", whilst Sharma himself said "I consider art to be an expression of mental peace and balance ...".

In 1975 Sharma was awarded a British Council scholarship to work and study at Middlesex Polytechnic, the University of London. In London, he reconnected with Maria Souza (Souza’s first wife) and showed his new works at her Arts 38 gallery at the India Tea Centre in London in 1976 (alongside Souza, Ribeiro and two other artists). F.N. Souza too continued to be a great supporter. Prakash Parimal in 1981 wrote an essay in the Lalit Kala Contemporary magazine on ‘Mohan Sharma: Painter of Silent Cityscapes’ and a 2017 book by Dr Sangeeta on the Development of Modern Art Criticism in India described his cityscapes as confirming Sharma’s status as the ‘topmost cubist artist’ in India and quoted M. F. Hussain praising Sharma’s ‘superfine qualities of geometrical perfection’. Dr Sangeeta noted that major paintings included ‘Bridge over Thames’, ‘Sand Dunes’ and ‘Golden Fort of Jaisalmer’. Sharma’s early death with cancer sadly cut off a very promising career.
