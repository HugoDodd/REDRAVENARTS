Nasser Azam

b.1963

Artist Biography

Naser Azam was born in Jhelum, Pakistan and moved to London with his family in 1970. He started painting seriously in 1980, despite undertaking a business degree at Birmingham University, and by 1983 had exhibited widely in the West Midlands, including a solo show at the Barber Institute. Early works from this period explored the themes of family and migration and two of these are now in the permanent collection of the Ben Uri Gallery in London. Azam worked successfully in finance and embarked on a period of extensive travelling in Japan, the United States and Europe before returning to London in 2006 to concentrate on his wide-ranging and varied artistic practice. He became known for large-scale sculptures, the two most prominent being sited on Westminster Bridge and near the City Airport in London but also created art in extreme climatic conditions from Antarctica to in zero gravity environments. In addition, in 2015 he painted the official portrait of Nobel Prize winner Malala Yousafzai. In 2019 Azam had a major solo exhibition at the Saatchi Gallery showcasing large scale paintings produced in the Himalayan mountains of Pakistan. As well as his many exhibitions in London Azam also had a solo show in Istanbul, Turkey in 2008. His work is in a number of British public institutions and also in many prestigious private collections.
