Roop Krishna

1901–1968

Artist Biography

Roop Krishna was born in Lahore, then India and now Pakistan. He studied at Santiniketan in rural Bengal under Abanindranath Tagore and Nandal Bose, initially painting Bengal-style watercolours, but with Bose introducing more modern thoughts and techniques. Later in the 1920s he absorbed many Western influences and ideas, first at the Ecole des Beaux Arts, Paris and then at the Royal College of Art (RCA). At the RCA Roop met fellow artist Mary Oldfield. Roop returned to Lahore in 1930 before Mary followed two years later. When the couple married in 1936 Mary took the Indian name Indumati Roop Krishna. They met other artists based around the Lahore School of Fine Arts and both taught classes there. Roop and Mary worked in a style that combined Western artistic influences with Indian techniques and subject matter, though Roop’s work tended more towards the abstract. Roop and Mary shared a joint exhibition in1938 at the Punjab Literary Leage in Lahore and they co-authored in 1940 and 1943 two volumes, ‘Art and Life’ and ‘Echo and Ego’, that were influential arguments for a universal artistic sensibility and for the need for Indian painters to pursue artistic routes that could break with earlier traditions. Indeed, Roop Krishna can be seen as a key force behind the introduction of modern art into Lahore. The couple though, hoping for more exposure for their art, returned permanently to London in 1946 and although he contributed to a mixed exhibition at Leicester Galleries in 1949 Roop seems to have had a much lower artistic profile there than in his days in India. The Kasturbhai Lalbhai Museum in Ahmedabad, Gujarat has a 1920 watercolour by Krishna sent as a postcard to his teacher Tagore, and a 1933 lithograph by Roop Krishna is in the British Museum collection.
