Siramdasu Venkata Rama Rao

b. 1936

Artist Biography

Siramdasu Venkata Rama Rao was born in Gudivada, India in 1936. After a finance degree at Andhra University in 1955, he gained a government diploma in Fine Arts from Kalakshetra in Chennai. Rao then joined the Madras School of Arts and Crafts, also in Chennai. He graduated in 1959 and after the award of a Government of India research fellowship went to New Delhi, where he stayed until 1962. A Commonwealth Fellowship that year took him to England and the Slade School of Fine Art in London where he remained until 1965. In London, Rao wanted ‘to challenge Western artists in their own form’, inventing a new painting technique by applying paint directly on boards and using oil paints to give the illusion of water colour painting. His work took on a vibrant form of cubism. Rao joined the Indian Painters Collective (IPC), an association for Indian artists living and working in London which in 1964 held an exhibition, Six Indian Painters, at India House, London. In 1965, the same year he graduated from the Slade, Rao showed in the Second Commonwealth Biennial of Abstract Art Exhibition at the Commonwealth Institute, London, where he was selected as ‘The Most Outstanding Artist in the Commonwealth’. Over the following two years, he taught painting and drawing at London County Council.

In 1967 Rao moved to the USA and completed a Master of Fine Arts course at the University of Cincinnati two years later, concurrently teaching there. He then became Professor of Fine Arts at Western Kentucky University, later moving to Chicago. In 2001, for his contributions to the field of art, Rao was awarded the Padma Shri, the fourth-highest civilian award in his native country, by the Government of India. He returned to India two years later. Selected artworks and items from his archive were shown in London’s Grosvenor Gallery at The Roots of the Indian Artists’ Collectives exhibition in 2019. Rao’s work is held in the UK public domain in the collections of the UCL Art Museum, and the V&A in London.
