Teng-Hiok Chiu

1903-1972

Artist Biography

Teng-Hiok Chiu was born in Xiamen, Fujian province to a wealthy tea merchant family. In 1920, aged 17, he was sent to Harvard and then the School of the Museum of Fine Arts in Boston. He relocated to France in 1923, studying briefly at the Ecole des Beaux Arts in Paris before attending London University in 1924. From 1925-30 he studied under George Clausen at the Royal Academy of Arts Schools, where he was awarded both the prestigious Turner Gold Medal and the Royal Academy Scholarship for Landscape Painting. His work was selected for the Royal Academy Annual Exhibition on six occasions between 1927 and 1938, and he became the first foreigner ever granted membership into the Royal Society of British Artists.

A turning point came in 1929-30 while studying Chinese classical paintings at the British Museum under Laurence Binyon, leading him to incorporate an Eastern aesthetic into his naturalistic landscapes. Chiu traveled extensively in the 1930s across the Far East, Southeast Asia, Europe, and North Africa before settling permanently in the United States in 1938, where he became friendly with Georgia O’Keeffe. Though his work gradually fell out of favor during his lifetime, a landmark memorial exhibition in 2003 at the Frye Art Museum in Seattle helped restore his legacy. Bradford Art Gallery in the UK holds his work.
