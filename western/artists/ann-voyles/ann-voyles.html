Ann Voyles

1937 – 2014

Artist Biography

Ann Voyles (nee Simpson) was born in Edmond in Oklahoma, taking the married name of her Air America and CIA pilot husband James Voyles. A largely self-taught artist, Ann Voyles moved to Laos in 1963 and lived there during a time of war, US military intervention and then revolution, experiencing both danger and poverty before her return to the United States in the mid-1970s. Voyles sketched and painted throughout her time in Laos, her works offering a fascinating glimpse of the country and its people in that period. She illustrated a Laotian children’s book and had a solo art exhibition there in which all 81 of her works in various media sold. Her main customers were international and American diplomats and corporate executives as well as local royal families. Voyles also took part in official art cultural exchanges involving the Lao Art Society. On her return to the United States, following the Communist takeover of Laos, she embarked on a career in teaching and design in Edmond, her art taking rather a back seat in subsequent years.
