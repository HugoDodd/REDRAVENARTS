Daniel Harris (Zev)

1914 – 1987

Artist Biography

Daniel Harris was born in the United States. He studied at the National Academy of Design in New York and at the Roerich Museum in Hungary but his art was far from academic and was closer to that of self-taught outsider artists. He exhibited at the Abstract and Surrealist Art in America exhibition in San Francisco in 1944. In the late 1940s Harris adopted the name Zev, which means wolf in Hebrew, and in 1953 he decamped to Europe. There his other-worldly art, often of birds, the moon and various fantasy creatures, struck a chord. Zev’s use of intense monochrome scribbling in the 1950s evolved towards a simpler and very colourful and cheerful style by the 1970s. He lived for some time in Paris and then in Rome, exhibiting at the Espace Gallery in Amsterdam, Galerie Obelisco in Rome and Galerie Furstenburg in Paris amongst others. Zev lived a flamboyant lifestyle and was friends with Paul Klee and Pavel Tretchikoff in Paris before moving to Rome where his brightly decorated apartment was the centre of a homosexual social community that included Muriel Spark. Over the years he decorated in vivid colours the exteriors of a number of houses, including his own in Big Sur, California (later demolished) and a villa in Spain and painted Henry Miller’s piano in orange, yellow and bright blue. His scrapbooks from 1955-1963 are in the Smithsonian Archives.
