Edith Rimmington

1902 – 1988

Artist Biography

Edith Rimmington was a Surrealist artist, both in watercolours, poetry and, latterly, photographs. Relatively neglected in her lifetime, her importance to the movement is increasingly recognised. Born in Leicester, she studied at Brighton School of Art. In 1937 she moved from Manchester to London and was accepted enthusiastically into the circle around E L T Mesens and the London Surrealist Group. She was much admired and encouraged by artists such as Edward Burra and her close friend John Banting. Her work was delicate and precise, yet poetic, notable elements being sex, death and the sea. She had exhibitions in London in 1940 and Paris 1947. From 1950 Rimmington concentrated on photography. She moved to Bexhill-on-Sea Sussex in later life. Like her friends Emmy Bridgewater and Conroy Maddox, Rimmington was one of the mainstays of surrealism in England for decades. Her work is held by the National Gallery of Scotland and was shown in the Mayor Gallery, London’s 2009 exhibition on British Surrealism.
