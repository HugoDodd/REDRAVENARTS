Enrique Arnal

1932 – 2016

Artist Biography

Enrique Arnal was born in Catavi, a mining community in the Bolivian Andes. He was largely self-taught as an artist, but a 1966-67 study scholarship took him to Paris and a later Fulbright scholarship to Virgina, USA. At various times he ran art galleries and taught at art schools but these were essentially sidelines to help fund his artistic output. Arnal had a first solo show in Cuzco, Peru in 1954 and later one-man shows in La Paz, Asuncion, Buenos Aires, Santiago and elsewhere in Latin America; in addition he exhibited in Paris, France and Washington DC and New York in the States. Arnal is considered a key figure in Bolivian modernism and his influences included both Picasso and Bacon. Arnal frequently returned to the subjects of bird and animals but their often tortured forms reflected a view of humanity perhaps shaped by political turbulence and periods of political exile. He was the subject of a documentary film in 2018, two years after his death, and a major retrospective of over 80 paintings and drawings in 2023 at the Fundacio Patino gallery in La Paz, which was accompanied by a book on his life and works. His paintings are held by permanent collections including the National Museum of Art in La Paz, Bolivia and the Organisation of American States Art Museum in Washington, USA.
