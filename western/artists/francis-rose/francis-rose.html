Francis Rose

1909 – 1979

Artist Biography

Francis Rose, who inherited a Scottish Baronetcy and independent wealth, was born in England but raised in southern France. In 1929 he painted with Christopher Wood in Brittany, the two reputedly being lovers, and in 1930 he shared his first exhibition with Salvador Dali in Paris. Rose lived in Paris from 1929-36, studying under Francis Picabia, and in 1931 he met Gertrude Stein who was to become a great friend and patron. Rose travelled extensively in the 1930s, living a somewhat sybaritic lifestyle, but achieved some success in his painting with shows in Paris, London and New York as well as set designs for Diaghilev’s Ballet Russes. His style – after initial forays into realism - encompassed elements of surrealism and expressionism, utilising a deft sketching ability. His lifestyle, despite continued artistic success, led to a colourful autobiography published in 1961 and bankruptcy. Even so, Rose had a major retrospective in 1966 at the South London Art Gallery and England & Co, also in London, held a posthumous retrospective in 1988. Rose’s work is held in public collections at Yale University, Fine Arts Museums of San Francisco and the British Museum.
