Georgina Beier

1938 – 2021

Artist Biography

Georgina Beier, née Betts, was born in London in 1938. At age sixteen she enrolled in the Kingston Art School; however, she dropped out after fifteen months and in 1959 she moved to Nigeria where she met and married Ulli Beier, becoming his second wife. In 1964 Beier began conducting annual art workshops at the Mbari Mbayo Club (Osogbo, Nigeria), where she encouraged her students to be authentic, rather than becoming preoccupied with academic constraints. Famous artists who emerged from these workshops, such as Twins Seven Seven, Muraina Oyelami, and Rufus Ogundele, were, like Beier, never academically trained. Beier also participated in the theatre company of the Mbari Mbayo Club, painting backdrops and designing costumes for plays and religious ceremonies. Her own artwork at this time leant heavily on Yoruba iconography. Beier left Nigeria in 1966 along with her husband, later living in several different countries, including Papua New Guinea, Australia, India, and Germany. She continued to run workshops with local artists and maintained her own artistic practice, producing many works including paintings, sculptures, textiles, lithographs, furniture design, murals, mosaics, and illustrations for publications by Ulli Beier. The Tate Modern in London showed one of her works in its 2001 exhibition Century City: Lagos. Her work was also shown at Nigerian Modernism at the Tate Modern in London 2025-26.
