Yasmin Brandolini d’Adda

1929 – 2012

Artist Biography

Born in Cape Town in South Africa Yasmin Petersen studied art but abandoned it after marriage to Italian count Brandolino Brandilini d’Addo, moving to northern Italy in the 1950s and concentrating on raising their two sons. After a period of illness, however, she returned to painting around 1970 – initially in a figurative style painting landscapes and nudes, but increasingly moving towards pure and almost monochromatic abstracts. Her favoured media were watercolours, etching and collage, never painting in oils. Whilst she carried out some large scale installations, the bulk of her paintings were small scale and represented, apparently, short stories of her inner travels. Brandolini d’Adda had a first solo exhibition at Cavour Gallery in Milan in 1971 and this was followed by numerous other shows in Italy, mainly in Milan, Rome and Venice, until her final solo exhibition at Bevilacqua La Masa in Venice in 2000. During this period she also had one-person exhibitions in Paris, Washington, Miami and New York, in addition to solo shows in Cape Town and Johannesburg in her South African homeland.
